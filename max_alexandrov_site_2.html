About

A high-school researcher who treats biology as something you do with people, not just for a grade.

My work lives at the meeting point of data-driven biology and hands-on conservation. On the computational side, I have built bioinformatics pipelines at Cambridge's Teichmann Lab, taught myself to apply zero-shot machine learning to discover novel anti-CRISPR proteins, and worked daily with the modern protein-design stack: AlphaFold, ESM-2, ProteinMPNN, and RFdiffusion.

On the ground, I have spent three years leading field conservation alongside the villages near my school, from mapping and removing invasive species with our local government to investigating water pollution from textile manufacturing. The throughline is simple: I like understanding a problem deeply, building something real to address it, and making sure it keeps running after I step away.

I recently graduated from UWC Changshu China, and I am excited to put AI to work on problems that matter for real communities.

Explorations

Higher-risk personal R&D. Neither shipped, and that is the point.

These are the ambitious side projects where I learned the de novo protein-design and structural-modeling stack. With both, I built my own version and compared it honestly against the best existing work. Theirs was clearly better, so I studied what they did, kept what I had learned, and carried it forward.

OrthCom-immuno

Shelved
Personal research · methods & validation

Set out to measure how badly the field's standard shortcut, swapping a non-canonical amino acid for its nearest canonical residue, distorts MHC-I antigen-presentation predictions, aiming for a position-resolved map of the liability the shortcut misses. The plan validated against PTM-modified HLA-A*02:01 peptides with measured IC50s before extending to synthetic aromatic ncAAs, built on an RDKit conformer plus Psi4 DFT scoring core. I shelved it once I found existing work already did this better, but I came away understanding the structural modeling deeply.

MHC-IncAARDKit / Psi4 DFT

OrthCom

Shelved
Personal research · de novo design

An attempt to design a de novo bioorthogonal receptor that could selectively recognize a non-canonical amino acid "key" by an affinity gap, using protein hallucination and the modern design stack. After building it, I compared my version against the strongest published work, saw theirs was better, and folded what I had learned into OrthCom-immuno. It is where I picked up the full de novo toolchain end to end.
